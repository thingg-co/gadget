The device

Every part is there for a reason.

A phone-shaped slab of printed aluminum and e-ink. No touchscreen, no app store, no subscription. The parts that matter are the parts that last.

  • 4.2" e-ink display. Sharp in sunlight, draws nothing when static.
  • Two LoRa radios. One carries the control plane, one carries data. Frequency-diverse at 915 MHz.
  • A 2.4 GHz data plane. 802.15.4 mesh plus Bluetooth LE with 20 concurrent links per node.
  • GPS, buttons, buzzer. Location, input, alerts. No touchscreen, no app store.
  • 3000 mAh cell. The radios sleep unless they have to wake up.
  • USB-C charging. A $52 pile of parts in a printed slab.

The network

The mesh is the network.

Every Gadget is a node. Text and telemetry ride LoRa for kilometers. Bigger data rides 2.4 GHz when nodes are near each other. Home base stations are exit nodes, and the internet arrives at the edge, not the center.

  • Control plane on LoRa. Topology, routes, keys, and short text at any range. It always exists.
  • Data plane on 2.4 GHz. Thread-style mesh at 250 kbps, plus 20 simultaneous Bluetooth links. Density becomes bandwidth.
  • Home base stations. The same core with a 16-channel concentrator and Ethernet. They run the controller and reach the internet for everyone nearby.
  • Exit nodes are volunteers. Traffic egresses on the operator's own connection, end-to-end encrypted by default.

Power

Measured in months, not hours.

Deep sleep is about five microamps. At that rate the battery outlives itself. The e-ink only draws during a refresh, and a LoRa burst is a millisecond event.

years

Idle, radios off. The limit is self-discharge, not the cell.

months

Normal use at 1% radio duty. Charge it monthly, forget it.

days

Heavy use: continuous mesh, GPS, screen refreshes.

The numbers are estimates for rev A, to be measured on real boards. The point of the design is that the radio owns the power budget, and the radio sleeps.

Open hardware. Private by default.

Built from zero, kept by its users.

Nothing inherited, nothing licensed away. The schematics, the firmware, and the docs are all open, and all noncommercial by force of license.

Open

Hardware under CERN-OHL-NC. Firmware under PolyForm Noncommercial. Docs under CC BY-NC 4.0. Anyone can read, build, and repair.

Private

End-to-end encryption on every hop. Keys live in secure storage on the device. No telemetry, no phone-home, no kill switch.

Independent

Exit nodes are run by the people who use them. The software is built so the network survives without a company behind it.
